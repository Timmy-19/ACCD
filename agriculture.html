Why ACC deaminase matters in agriculture

When plants experience abiotic or biotic stress, they up-regulate ACC synthase and produce a damaging second "stress-ethylene" peak that triggers senescence, chlorosis, leaf abscission, and yield loss. Rhizospheric and endophytic bacteria carrying acdS intercept ACC exuded from roots and convert it to α-ketobutyrate + NH₃ — depleting the substrate pool for ethylene biosynthesis and rescuing the plant.

The plant–microbe model

  1. Stress signal (NaCl, drought, hypoxia, pathogen, heavy metal) → plant up-regulates SAM synthase.
  2. SAM → ACC synthase → ACC (precursor of ethylene).
  3. ACC diffuses out of root tissue into the rhizosphere or endosphere.
  4. PGPB take up ACC and cleave it via AcdS → α-ketobutyrate + NH₃ (used as N source).
  5. Lowered ACC pool → suppressed second ethylene peak → plant resumes growth.
  6. IAA crosstalk: bacterial IAA stimulates plant ACC synthase, but AcdS blocks the ethylene feedback that would shut down IAA signalling — the two activities synergise.

Ethylene is bioactive at concentrations as low as 0.05 µL/L; the typical plant response is triggered at < 1.0 ppm. Fruit-ripening ethylene reaches 200 µL/L.

Activity thresholds & field considerations

  • Functional cut-off: > 20 nmol α-ketobutyrate / mg protein / h is the de-facto "effective PGPB" threshold (Glick 2014; Penrose & Glick assay).
  • Induction substrate: ACC ≥ 100 nM; basal activity ≤ 5 % of induced.
  • Optimal pH / T: 8.0–8.5 and 25–30 °C — well-matched to many soil environments.
  • Cytoplasmic localisation: ACCD is intracellular; ACC enters via passive diffusion or a transporter.
  • Microbial population stress effect: 10⁸–10⁹ cells / g (healthy) → 10⁴ cells / g (saline soil) — inoculation can re-establish PGPB density.
  • Rhizobia caveat: ACCD activity in symbionts is 2–10 % of free-living PGPB but functionally sufficient because rhizobia sit inside nodules in tight contact with the host.

acdS expression in vivo

  • acdR: Lrp-family regulator divergently transcribed upstream of acdS; senses leucine (a downstream catabolite of α-KB).
  • Promoter elements: FNR box (microaerobic activation), Lrp/AcdR site, CRP site.
  • Inducer: ACC (≥ 100 nM); basal ≤ 5 %.
  • Symbiotic rewiring: in Mesorhizobium loti, acdS is moved under nifA / σ⁵⁴ control — co-induced with nitrogen fixation.
  • Horizontal transfer: rare; acdS shows mostly vertical inheritance, consistent with deep evolutionary co-option.
  • Survey statistics: ~12 % of 233 Saskatchewan rhizobial strains are acdS⁺; 18/18 Portuguese chickpea Mesorhizobium isolates are acdS⁺.
